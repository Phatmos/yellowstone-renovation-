Water at an entry threshold can come from several places. A worn sweep may let wind-driven rain past the door; an out-of-level sill or missing water-management detail can direct moisture beneath it; and water may travel from siding or trim above the opening. The right repair starts with finding the route, not simply adding more caulk.

If you are selecting a new door, our [fiberglass-versus-steel entry door guide](/blog/fiberglass-vs-steel-entry-door-lexington-ky-2026/) covers materials. This article addresses the opening itself and the details to discuss during an exterior-door estimate in Lexington.

## Observe when and where the water appears

Photograph the threshold, adjacent flooring, jamb bottoms, and exterior trim during or immediately after rain. Does water show up only when wind blows toward the door? Is the top of the frame wet? Is one jamb soft? Does the problem occur when a nearby downspout overflows? These observations help narrow the source before any trim is removed.

Keep water away from damaged interior finishes while arranging an inspection. If the flooring is soft or repeatedly wet, the repair may extend beyond the visible door slab.

## Check the door's moving seals

Inspect the bottom sweep, perimeter weatherstripping, door alignment, and latch engagement. A door that no longer closes evenly can leave a gap even with new seals. Replacing a sweep may help when the opening and sill are sound, but it cannot repair a deteriorated substrate or missing flashing below the threshold.

## Look beyond the threshold

Check whether the exterior landing directs water toward the house, whether the sill has support, and whether trim and siding above are intact. Water can enter at a higher joint and emerge at the floor. The [siding transition guide](/blog/siding-flashing-around-windows-doors-lexington-ky/) explains why the barrier and flashing around openings matter. If nearby windows show moisture, use the [window condensation location guide](/blog/window-condensation-location-lexington-ky/) to separate glass fog from a wall leak.

## Slab, prehung unit or opening repair?

A slab-only change keeps the existing frame. A prehung replacement includes a new slab and frame, but may still require correcting the rough opening, sill support, flashing, trim, or damaged surrounding material. Ask the installer what will be removed, what can be inspected, how hidden rot will be documented, and how the new assembly will shed water outward.

## Questions for an estimate

- Is the existing frame and sill staying or being replaced?
- How will the installer diagnose the leak path before sealing it?
- Are trim, flashing, floor repair, paint, and disposal included?
- How will concealed damage be approved and priced?
- Will the new threshold work with the existing exterior landing and door swing?

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I fix a leaking threshold with caulk?

Sealant may be one part of a repair, but it can also trap water if applied across a drainage path. Identify the source and follow the door system's installation details.

### Does a new door automatically stop a leak?

No. If the water originates in the surrounding wall, exterior grade, or flashing, those conditions need attention as part of the work.
