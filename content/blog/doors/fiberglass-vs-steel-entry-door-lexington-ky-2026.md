# Fiberglass vs. Steel Entry Doors in Lexington, KY

A new front door changes curb appeal immediately, but the slab material is only one part of a successful replacement. The frame, sill, weatherstripping, flashing, lock preparation and condition of the surrounding wall all affect how the finished door operates and keeps weather outside.

For Lexington homeowners, fiberglass and steel are two common exterior-door choices. Both can work well when the unit is properly specified and installed. The better option depends on appearance, exposure, maintenance expectations and budget.

## Fiberglass entry doors

Fiberglass doors are available in smooth painted finishes and textured surfaces designed to resemble wood. They do not rust, and many product lines offer a wide range of panel styles, glass packages and stain or paint options.

Fiberglass is often attractive when the front entrance is a major design feature and the homeowner wants more style choices without using a traditional wood slab.

### Fiberglass may be a good fit if you value

- Wood-look textures or a broad range of styles
- Resistance to rust
- Low routine surface maintenance
- Decorative glass and premium design options

## Steel entry doors

Steel doors can provide a clean, durable exterior at a competitive price point. They are commonly factory-primed or prefinished and are available in many standard panel and glass configurations.

The finish should be maintained if it is scratched or damaged because exposed steel can corrode. The actual performance of the door also depends heavily on the frame, sill, weatherstripping and installation—not simply the metal skin.

### Steel may be a good fit if you value

- Straightforward painted styles
- A strong value-oriented option
- Broad availability of common configurations
- A crisp, simple appearance

## Do not choose the slab before checking the opening

An older exterior door may have problems that a new slab alone will not fix. Look for soft jamb material, a damaged sill, daylight around the perimeter, recurring water staining, difficult locking or a frame that is visibly out of square.

If the frame is deteriorated, a prehung replacement may be more appropriate than trying to reuse the existing jamb. The installer should evaluate the opening and explain what will remain and what will be replaced.

## Glass changes privacy, light and price

A solid door provides maximum privacy. Half-lite, three-quarter-lite and full-lite configurations bring more daylight into the entry but change the appearance and cost. Decorative, textured and privacy glass can balance natural light with visibility from outside.

For doors exposed to strong sun, discuss the manufacturer's requirements for the exact finish and storm-door configuration before ordering.

## Installation details that matter

A quality exterior-door replacement should address sill support, a square and plumb frame, proper fastening, insulation around the jamb, exterior water management, trim and sealant, smooth operation, consistent reveals, weatherstripping contact and lock alignment.

If nearby siding is also deteriorated, coordinate the work so exterior flashing and trim are not repeatedly removed. Our guide to [siding replacement warning signs](/blog/signs-your-siding-needs-replacement-lexington-ky-2026/) explains what to inspect around the rest of the exterior.

## What should be listed in an entry-door estimate?

Ask for the manufacturer and product line, slab material, prehung versus slab-only installation, door size, swing, glass configuration, jamb material, sill, hardware allowance, interior and exterior trim, painting or factory finish, disposal and the process for concealed damage.

A complete specification prevents a common problem: comparing two quotes that appear to cover the same “front door” but actually include very different products and finish work.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is fiberglass better than steel?
Not universally. Fiberglass offers broad style options and will not rust; steel can be an excellent value with a durable painted surface. Installation quality and the complete door system matter in either case.

### Should I replace the frame with the door?
If the jamb, sill or opening has damage or poor alignment, a full prehung replacement may be the better approach. A site inspection can determine what is reusable.

### Can a new door improve drafts?
It can when the existing door has poor seals or alignment, but the new unit must be sized, installed and weatherstripped correctly. Drafts can also originate elsewhere in the wall assembly.
