# Deck Ledger Flashing in Lexington, KY: What Keeps Water Out

The connection between a deck and a house deserves more attention than almost any finish detail. When an attached deck uses a ledger, the assembly has to carry structural loads while also directing rain away from the wall. A beautiful composite surface cannot compensate for a poorly detailed connection.

## Why the ledger area is vulnerable

The ledger sits where exterior cladding, wall sheathing, fasteners and deck framing meet. Water that gets behind the siding needs a deliberate path back out. Simply applying exterior caulk along the top edge is not a complete water-management strategy.

A sound scope should identify the existing wall condition, how the ledger will be attached, how flashing integrates with the weather-resistive layer, and what happens at the ends of the ledger.

## What homeowners should ask to see

Before the deck boards cover the framing, ask for photos of the ledger and flashing. Look for a continuous drainage strategy rather than isolated beads of sealant. The exact assembly depends on the wall and cladding, so the contractor should adapt the detail to the house rather than force one detail everywhere.

If siding is being replaced too, coordinate both scopes. Our guide to [flashing around windows and doors](/blog/siding-flashing-around-windows-doors-lexington-ky/) explains the same basic principle: layers should shed water outward.

## Rebuilds deserve extra investigation

Removing an old deck can reveal soft sheathing, damaged trim, previous fastener holes or an improvised ledger detail. Those conditions can change the scope. A responsible estimate should distinguish visible work from concealed repairs that cannot be priced accurately until demolition.

## FAQ

### Can flashing be added without removing siding?
Sometimes, but the correct approach depends on the existing cladding and wall assembly. The goal is integration with the drainage plane, not merely covering the joint.

### Is ledger flashing only important on wood decks?
No. Composite decking changes the walking surface, not the need to protect the house connection.

### Should I photograph the framing before decking goes down?
Yes. Progress photos create a useful record of hidden details such as blocking, connectors and flashing.
